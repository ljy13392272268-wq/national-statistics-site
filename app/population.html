Population

340 million people — and still growing

The population rose from 321.82 million in 2015 to 340.00 million in 2024. Growth slowed to 0.16% in 2021, the slowest year in the series, then rebounded to 0.96% in 2024. The population is also ageing: the share aged 65 and above rose from 14.3% in 2015 to 17.9% in 2024, while the fertility rate fell to 1.63 births per woman.

Source: World Bank Open Data, 2015–2024.

Still growing

Total population, 2015–2024. Growth slowed through the decade to 0.16% in 2021, then rebounded to 0.96% in 2024 — the fastest year in the series.

Source: World Bank Open Data, indicator SP.POP.TOTL, 2015–2024.

An older country

Age structure in 2015 and in 2024. The working-age share fell from 66.5% to 64.7%; the 65-and-over share rose from 14.3% to 17.9%.

Source: World Bank Open Data, indicators SP.POP.0014.TO.ZS, SP.POP.1564.TO.ZS and SP.POP.65UP.TO.ZS.

Four in five live in cities

Urban population as a share of the total, 2015–2024: it has hovered near 80% all decade, at 80.1% in 2024. The fertility rate fell over the same years, from 1.84 to 1.63 births per woman.

Source: World Bank Open Data, indicators SP.URB.TOTL.IN.ZS and SP.DYN.TFRT.IN, 2015–2024.

Population data, 2015–2024

Source: World Bank Open Data. Population is shown in millions; growth, urban share and age shares are percentages.

Reading the numbers. Births fell from 12.4 per 1,000 people in 2015 to 10.6 in 2024, while deaths rose from 8.44 to 9.0 per 1,000 — and spiked to 10.4 in 2021, the pandemic year. That spike is why population growth nearly stopped in 2021 before rebounding.
