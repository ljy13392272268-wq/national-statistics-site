Economy

A $29.30 trillion economy

The GDP of the United States reached $29.30 trillion in 2024, growing 2.8% that year. GDP per person is $86,170 — the same figure adjusted for purchasing power, because the dollar is the PPP reference currency. Prices rose 2.9% in 2024, after 8.0% in 2022, and trade is worth 25.0% of GDP.

Source: World Bank Open Data, 2015–2024.

GDP: ten years of growth

GDP in current US dollars, 2015–2024. The economy grew from $18.30 trillion to $29.30 trillion — with a dip to $21.38 trillion in the pandemic year 2020, then a sharp rebound to $23.73 trillion in 2021.

Source: World Bank Open Data, indicator NY.GDP.MKTP.CD, 2015–2024.

Growth, and the inflation spike

Real GDP growth, 2015–2024. Growth ran near 2–3% until 2019, fell to −2.1% in 2020, rebounded to 6.2% in 2021, and settled near 2.5–2.9% after that. Inflation stayed below 2.5% until 2020, then jumped to 8.0% in 2022 before easing to 2.9% in 2024.

Source: World Bank Open Data, indicators NY.GDP.MKTP.KD.ZG and FP.CPI.TOTL.ZG, 2015–2024.

Richer per person, and trading less

GDP per capita rose from $56,849 in 2015 to $86,170 in 2024. Trade — exports plus imports — slipped from 27.7% of GDP to 25.0%; in 2024 exports were 11.0% of GDP and imports 14.0%.

Source: World Bank Open Data, indicators NY.GDP.PCAP.CD and NE.TRD.GNFS.ZS, 2015–2024.

Reading the numbers. GDP in current dollars mixes real growth with price moves — that is why 2021 and 2022 look so steep. The growth chart above is the real (inflation-adjusted) rate. And the PPP figure is identical to the market-rate figure for the United States: the US dollar is the reference currency for purchasing-power parity, so the two series coincide by construction.
