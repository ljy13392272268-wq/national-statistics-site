Health

78.9 years — after a pandemic dip

A child born in the United States in 2024 can expect to live 78.9 years: 81.4 for a girl, 76.5 for a boy. Life expectancy fell to 76.3 years in 2021, then recovered. Infant mortality edged down from 5.8 to 5.5 deaths per 1,000 live births. Health spending was 16.7% of GDP in 2023, the latest year reported.

Source: World Bank Open Data, 2015–2024.

Longer lives — with a dip

Life expectancy at birth, 2015–2024. Women live about 4.9 years longer than men. It fell from 78.8 years in 2019 to 76.3 in 2021, then recovered to 78.9 by 2024.

Source: World Bank Open Data, indicators SP.DYN.LE00.IN, SP.DYN.LE00.FE.IN and SP.DYN.LE00.MA.IN, 2015–2024.

Fewer children dying — slowly

Infant and under-5 mortality, per 1,000 live births, 2015–2024. Both fell only slightly over the decade, and have been flat at 5.5 and 6.5 since 2019.

Source: World Bank Open Data, indicators SP.DYN.IMRT.IN and SH.DYN.MORT, 2015–2024.

What health costs

Current health expenditure as a share of GDP, 2015–2023 (2024 not yet reported). It spiked to 18.5% in the pandemic year 2020, then settled back near 16.7%.

Source: World Bank Open Data, indicator SH.XPD.CHEX.GD.ZS, 2015–2023.

Health data, 2015–2024

Source: World Bank Open Data. Skilled birth attendance is last reported for 2020 (99.1%); health spending is last reported for 2023.

Modelled estimates. The mortality figures are the United Nations Inter-agency Group for Child Mortality Estimation values as carried by the World Bank, and the life expectancy figures are the World Bank's series — they are estimates built from census and survey data, not headcounts, which is why they are revised from time to time.
